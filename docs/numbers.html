How the method arrives at it

Step 3 starts from the Annex B pattern, the published argument pattern for AI components in road vehicles, and keeps its goals. A claim from another standard is attached to an existing goal when it serves the same purpose. A new goal is created only when nothing in the base fits. That happened twice: G7 and G8 each claim that a residual risk is acceptable (SOTIF and cybersecurity), which is a judgement, not a verification activity, so neither belongs under G5. G9, re-assurance after an update or retraining, is drawn but left undeveloped, because no standard prescribes the complete workflow.

Chosen, or found?

The base pattern is a choice; the nine follows from it. With a different base (AMLAS or SACE, for example) the goals would be numbered and cut differently. What the papers rely on is not the count but where the standards meet, and that is checked separately: see the findings card below.

How the method arrives at it

G5 claims that verification and validation are sufficient. It is the one goal where every normative standard contributes, and each asks for its own evidence: structural coverage of the non-AI code (ISO 26262), scenario coverage against triggering conditions (ISO 21448), a statistical uncertainty score (ISO/PAS 8800), and an attack success rate from penetration testing (ISO/SAE 21434).

Chosen, or found?

Found. One kind of evidence per contributing standard, and the four measure on scales that do not convert into one another. That is the central result: no clause says how to combine them.

Step 4 examines every goal where claims from two or more standards meet (G1, G2, G4, G5, G6, G8). A decision point is recorded wherever the standards leave a choice to the project instead of prescribing one. Terminological ones (two words for one thing) and methodological ones (two methods for one aim) a project can settle by agreeing on a vocabulary or a method. How the method arrives at it

Step 5 sorts what the integrated argument exposes into two kinds. An integration-induced finding exists only between the scopes of the standards, so no reader of one standard alone can see it: the adversarial-SOTIF boundary that belongs to neither G7 nor G8 (F-3), and the release decision that has to combine all four kinds of evidence (F-4). An open methodological problem is a framework the standards do prescribe, with no published method yet for the numbers it needs (F-1, F-2, F-5). The code writes Gap-1 to Gap-5 for F-1 to F-5.

Chosen, or found?

Found, and tested against the obvious objection that a different argument structure would make the integration-induced findings disappear. They were derived under two structures, Activity-based (this work) and Concern hierarchy (Warg & Skoglund): both arise under each. The derivation reports the result either way; it is not tuned.
