Design time and operation: the two papers side by side

The WAISE paper (Integrating Cybersecurity Into the AI Safety Assurance Argument, WAISE 2026) covers design time. The position paper (Operational Safety and Cybersecurity Assurance of AI-Based Perception in Highly Automated Driving, SAFECOMP 2026) covers operation.

What this page does. Each paper reports, for its own phase, that results from several standards have to support one claim, are expressed on different measurement scales, and that no clause says how to combine them. This page sets the two reports side by side. The comparison is a synthesis drawn in this repository; neither paper states it, and the page adds no evidence beyond what each paper says.

Design time: G5, the verification and validation (V&V) goal

Suppose every prescribed verification activity is complete. Is the V&V evidence, taken together, sufficient?

  • MC/DC coverage of the surrounding non-AI software
    Scale: MC/DC coverage ratio. Source: ISO 26262.
  • SOTIF triggering condition coverage in scenario testing
    Scale: triggering-condition coverage fraction. Source: ISO 21448.
  • Deep ensemble disagreement, measured by AUROC
    Scale: AUROC of ensemble disagreement. Source: ISO/PAS 8800.
  • Adversarial penetration testing against LiDAR spoofing
    Scale: attack success rate. Source: ISO/SAE 21434.

No standard defines how the disjoint evidence types combine to support a single sufficiency claim at G5.

The concern-specific results meet at the top claim on different measurement scales, and no rule combines them.

Conditions that operation adds (position paper):

  • Evidence arrives asynchronously
  • The component changes under updates
  • The reporting duty requires a current judgement on demand

Position paper, missing step 2 (resolution): no clause resolves the concern-specific results into one judgement on the assurance argument. The four scales above are the paper's examples. Source: Position paper, SAFECOMP 2026 (to appear).

What the two phases have in common

  • Each assurance domain or concern completes its own activity within its own scope
  • The results are expressed on different measurement scales
  • The results have to support a single claim
  • No clause in the standards examined says how to combine them

What differs

  • At design time the question arises before release and again after each modification (G9); in operation it recurs whenever a runtime monitor output is abnormal
  • At design time the evidence is assembled deliberately; in operation it arrives asynchronously and the component may have changed under an update
  • Operation carries a reporting duty that requires a current judgement on demand
  • In operation the cause of an anomaly is unknown, so it must first be assigned to a concern; at design time the cause is known, although its allocation between G7 and G8 can still be open (DP-5)

A second comparison: cases that fall between two standards

Before results can be combined, each case has to belong to a concern. Each paper reports a case that falls between the scopes of the standards (SOTIF is the safety of the intended functionality, ISO 21448).

Design time, F-3. Adversarial-SOTIF boundary unowned: adversarial inputs that exploit functional insufficiencies fall between G7 and G8 without belonging to either.

Operation, Missing step 1, assignment. An anomaly in operational evidence carries no concern label and does not show whether the cause falls under SOTIF, AI safety or cybersecurity, and no clause performs the assignment.

This comparison is looser than the first, because the two cases differ. In the WAISE paper's F-3 the cause is known: an adversarial input that exploits a functional insufficiency is both a threat scenario and a triggering condition, yet belongs to neither G7 nor G8, because no standard allocates it. In the position paper's missing step 1 the cause is unknown: the anomaly carries no concern label at all. What they share is that a case falls between the standards' scopes and no clause assigns it.
